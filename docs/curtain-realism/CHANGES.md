# Curtain realism overhaul

This round rebuilds the curtain's material, folds, lighting and opening. The
rest of the invitation is unchanged: the envelope, cover, music, sections and
timing hand-offs.

## Why the old curtain looked artificial

The audit was done in the code.

- **One gradient formula, repeated.** Each fold was an SVG quadrilateral
  filled with a gradient sampled from the same formula, so every fold had the
  same rounded profile and differed only in width. Six identical cylinders in
  a row read as stripes.
- **Texture was noise.** `feTurbulence` was spread evenly over the whole panel.
- **The panel moved rigidly.** It was a single element running a
  `translateX + scaleX` transition: a rectangle squashing toward the wall,
  with every fold squeezed by the same amount at the same moment.

## Technique: pre-rendered fabric, cut into folds

**The fabric is generated offline.** `scripts/curtain-fabric.py` builds each
panel as a heightfield of hanging cloth:

- irregular fold widths;
- folds pinched into pleats at the heading and flaring toward the hem;
- each fold deepening and relaxing along its drop;
- a few folds splitting partway down;
- a gentle belly across the whole panel;
- a rolled hem on the leading edge.

**One soft key light** from above and in front, toward the middle of the stage,
lights it:

- wrap diffuse;
- cavity occlusion, so valleys shade themselves;
- a weak satin sheen, only on the raised folds;
- the lamp's pool high on the cloth, falling off toward the wings, the floor
  and the heading.

**It is stored as a luminance map** (`public/kit/curtain-{narrow,wide}-{left,right}.webp`,
7–12 KB each). The page tints it with the theme's own cloth colour
(`--curtain-cloth`, from `ThemeStyle`) using `background-blend-mode:
luminosity`, so the shading is baked in but the colour still follows the
couple's theme. With the current theme that is champagne-taupe: mocha in the
valleys, champagne on the crests.

**The weave** is a seamless 2/2 twill tile with slubs in each thread
(`scripts/curtain-weave.py`, `curtain-weave.webp`, 8 KB). It is laid on in
`overlay`, sized in pixels and never stretched, so the thread scale is the
same on every screen.

**The two panels are different renders.** The right-hand panel is hung
mirrored, so both are lit from the centre but never fold alike.

**Fold count follows the panel's width:** 4 folds for panels narrower than
300 px, 6 for wider ones.

## The opening (3.4 s, including the settle)

`Drape.tsx` cuts each panel along its own valleys into one element per fold.
`curtainFolds.ts` is generated by the same script, so the cuts match the
rendered valleys exactly. While the curtain is closed, each fold tucks under
its neighbour, so no join shows.

`plan()` computes the movement once, when the curtain opens:

1. **Stillness.** The pull starts on a very soft curve.
2. **The pull.** The leading edge moves first. Each fold further out starts
   later (up to 0.42 s), so the gathering travels outward toward the wall.
3. **Gathering.** A fold is never stretched. Part of it turns under its
   neighbour and the rest is foreshortened. As it closes up it goes deeper in
   shade, and the fold sliding over it casts a soft shade onto it.
4. **The hem trails.** The bottom of each fold follows its heading on a
   slightly under-damped spring. It is drawn as a skew about the top edge,
   capped at 4°, so the opening is wider at the top than at the hem while the
   curtain moves.
5. **Settle.** The spring decays and the gathered cloth stays at the sides
   until the set is struck. The strike is unchanged: the cloth is drawn off and
   fades out, and is then unmounted.

**Implementation:**

- Playback uses the Web Animations API with transform and opacity only, so a
  phone composites the movement instead of repainting it.
- It runs once per opening (a ref guards against a second run).
- Its animations are cancelled when the component unmounts.
- Under reduced motion it jumps straight to the gathered pose.

The page's strike timers now read `CURTAIN_OPEN_MS` instead of a hard-coded
number.

## Tested (actually run)

These ran against a production build in Chromium (Playwright).

**Animation frames at 320×568, 390×844, 740×360 and 1280×800.** The harness
played the real opening from the envelope tap, froze the page clock and the
curtain's own animations, and seeked them. Each viewport has:

- closed;
- start (8 %);
- 25 %, 50 % and 75 %;
- open;
- the invitation after the curtain is struck.

These are the `before-*` and `after-*` sheets in this folder; the closed
curtain is also compared side by side in `closed-before-after-1280.png`.

**Results:**

- no console errors and no horizontal scroll at any viewport;
- the curtain unmounts after the strike;
- `tests/opening.mjs` passes 18/18;
- reduced motion opens straight into the gathered pose (fold transforms
  checked) and unmounts as before.

**Not tested:**

- Safari/WebKit and real phones. `background-blend-mode: luminosity`, `cqw`
  units and `clip-path` on moving layers are the parts worth checking on an
  iPhone.
- The uploaded-cloth path was not re-run.

## Known limitations

- **The 8 % frame looks nearly the same as closed.** That is the soft start
  working as intended, not a missing frame.
- **The gathered stack shows crisp vertical highlights.** They are the
  compressed crests. They read as gathered cloth in motion, and are most
  visible in a still frame.
- **The flat cast at the centre.** Just to the left of the closed centre join
  there is a flatter, shaded band: the left panel's leading fold under the
  right panel's cast shadow.
